§ 1

Continuous Morphisms of Sites

Last time a site was a category equipped with a notion of covering family. A map between sites is not extra structure — it is exactly what is needed to compare sheaves on two sites, and hence to say what pushforward and pullback of sheaves mean.

Definition — Continuous Morphism of Sites

Let $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{D}$ be sites. A continuous morphism of sites $f : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ is a functor

$$u : \mathcal{D} \longrightarrow \mathcal{C}$$

going in the reverse direction — exactly as a continuous map of spaces pulls back open sets — such that:

  • $u$ preserves fiber products: $u(Y_1 \times_Y Y_2) = u(Y_1) \times_{u(Y)} u(Y_2)$;
  • $u$ sends covering families to covering families: if $\{Y_\alpha \to Y\}$ covers $Y$, then $\{u(Y_\alpha) \to u(Y)\}$ covers $u(Y)$.

The direction reversal is the whole content of the definition, and it is worth stating as a slogan.

Slogan

A continuous morphism of sites is a functor that carries covers to covers and commutes with the fiber products in which gluing conditions live. Preserving covers is what lets a sheaf on the source be pulled back to a sheaf on the target; preserving fiber products is what keeps the gluing conditions themselves intact.

Example — Continuous Maps of Spaces Recovered

Let $f : X \to Y$ be a continuous map of topological spaces and put $u(V) = f^{-1}(V)$ for $V \subset Y$ open. Then $u$ preserves fiber products, because fiber products in $\mathrm{Open}(Y)$ are intersections and preimages commute with intersections:

$$f^{-1}(V_1 \cap V_2) = f^{-1}(V_1) \cap f^{-1}(V_2),$$

and $u$ sends covers to covers, because preimages commute with unions. So $f$ induces a continuous morphism of sites $X_{\mathrm{Zar}} \to Y_{\mathrm{Zar}}$, and the abstract definition recovers the familiar pushforward/pullback formalism for sheaves.

Example — The Sites of a Scheme Are Nested

For a scheme $X$, the identity functor on the underlying category gives continuous morphisms of sites

$$X_{\mathrm{Zar}} \longrightarrow X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}} \longrightarrow X_{\mathrm{Et}} \longrightarrow X_{\mathrm{fppf}}$$

(reading the arrows as maps of sites). Each inclusion of topologies goes the other way on underlying categories, and every Zariski cover is an étale cover, every étale cover is an fppf cover. This nesting is the reason a sheaf for a finer topology is automatically a sheaf for a coarser one: a condition imposed on more covering families is a stronger condition.

Remark — Terminology

A topos is a category of sheaves on a site, together with the extra structure — colimits, finite limits, and a way to glue — that makes it behave like a category of sheaves on a space. Different sites can present the same topos; in that case the associated categories of sheaves are equivalent, and the sites are two descriptions of one object. This is the sense in which Grothendieck topologies generalize topological spaces rather than merely decorate them.

§ 2

How to Check That Something Is a Sheaf

In practice one rarely checks the sheaf axiom on a site directly for every covering family. Two reduction principles do almost all the work.

(i) Reduce to the covers you understand. By the nesting of §1, being a sheaf for $X_{\mathrm{fppf}}$ implies being a sheaf for $X_{\mathrm{Et}}$ and $X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}$, which implies being a sheaf for $X_{\mathrm{Zar}}$. To verify the sheaf condition, it suffices to check it on a generating class of covers — for the étale site, on covers by a single étale map, and for the Zariski site, on covers by a finite family of open immersions.
(ii) Produce sheaves from representable functors. Rather than inventing presheaves and testing them, build them as representable functors $U \mapsto \operatorname{Hom}(U, Z)$ for a scheme $Z$, where the sheaf condition follows formally (§3 of the previous meeting, and §3 below).
Theorem — Representable Functors Are Sheaves, Revisited

Let $X$ be a scheme and $Y$ an $X$-scheme. Then the functor

$$h_Y : (X_{\mathrm{fppf}})^{\mathrm{op}} \longrightarrow \mathbf{Set}, \qquad U \longmapsto \operatorname{Hom}_X(U, Y)$$

is a sheaf on the fppf site $X_{\mathrm{fppf}}$; hence also on $X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}$ and on $X_{\mathrm{Zar}}$. Consequently $\mathbb{G}_m$, $\mu_n$, $\underline{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}$, and $\mathbb{P}^1$ all give sheaves on every one of these sites.

Why this is the right formal statement

Maps into $Y$ glue along a cover because they glue on the underlying topological spaces and the gluing is compatible with the structure sheaves; faithful flatness of finite presentation is what upgrades compatibility on overlaps to a genuine map. The point to retain is structural: a functor represented by a scheme is automatically a sheaf, so the supply of sheaves is not a problem. The interesting questions are about the ones that are not representable, such as the cokernels of natural maps (recall $\mathbb{G}_m \xrightarrow{n} \mathbb{G}_m$ from §6 of the previous meeting).

§ 3

Quasi-Coherent Sheaves Are Sheaves for the Étale and fppf Topologies

The next observation is the entry point to descent. A quasi-coherent sheaf on $X$ is, by definition, a sheaf for the Zariski topology. It turns out to satisfy the sheaf condition for far more covers than that.

Theorem

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a quasi-coherent sheaf on a scheme $X$. Then $\mathcal{F}$ is a sheaf on the fppf site $X_{\mathrm{fppf}}$, and hence also on $X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}$ and $X_{\mathrm{Zar}}$.

Equivalently, the evident functor is fully faithful:

$$\mathbf{QCoh}(X) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{Sh}(X_{\mathrm{fppf}}) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{Sh}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}).$$
Idea

A quasi-coherent sheaf is, Zariski-locally on $X$, a module $M$ over a ring $R$, and its sections over an $X$-scheme $U$ are $M \otimes_R \mathcal{O}(U)$. The sheaf condition for a cover $U' \to U$ becomes the statement that

$$M \;\longrightarrow\; M \otimes_R S \;\rightrightarrows\; M \otimes_R (S \otimes_R S)$$

is an equalizer whenever $R \to S$ is faithfully flat of finite presentation. That is exactly the affine lemma of §6 — the algebraic heart of descent. So the theorem is not an accident of the étale topology: it is the same flatness statement that makes descent work at all.

Remark — Why one cares

This is what allows us to speak of “the same” quasi-coherent sheaf in four different topologies, and hence to compare Zariski cohomology with étale and fppf cohomology of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules. It also means that the interesting part of descent is not that quasi-coherent sheaves are sheaves, but that they can be reconstructed from data downstairs — the subject of the rest of the lecture.

§ 4

Descent Data

Fix a cover $f : U \to X$. Given a sheaf on $X$, restricting along $f$ gives a sheaf on $U$; but restricting alone forgets information, because a cover has overlaps on which the restrictions must be compatible.

Definition — Descent Data for a Quasi-Coherent Sheaf

Let $f : U \to X$ be a cover and write $U^{\times_X 2} = U \times_X U$, $U^{\times_X 3} = U \times_X U \times_X U$, with the projections $p_1, p_2 : U \times_X U \to U$ and $p_{ij} : U^{\times_X 3} \to U \times_X U$. Descent data for a quasi-coherent sheaf along $f$ is a quasi-coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $U$, together with an isomorphism

$$\varphi : p_1^* \mathcal{F} \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; p_2^* \mathcal{F}$$

on $U \times_X U$ — the gluing isomorphism — satisfying the cocycle condition on $U^{\times_X 3}$:

$$p_{23}^*\varphi \,\circ\, p_{12}^*\varphi \;=\; p_{13}^*\varphi .$$

The three projections and their two ways around the triple overlap are worth reading as a picture: the cocycle condition says that the three identifications of $\mathcal{F}$ pulled back from the three copies of $U$ are consistent on the triple intersection. This is literally the same condition as the usual cocycle condition for transition functions of a vector bundle.

Translation

Descent data is “a sheaf on the cover plus consistent identifications on overlaps” — the same shape as gluing local data in the Zariski topology, but with the open cover replaced by an arbitrary cover in a Grothendieck topology.

Definition — Morphisms of Descent Data

A morphism of descent data $(\mathcal{F}, \varphi) \to (\mathcal{G}, \psi)$ is a morphism $h : \mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{G}$ of quasi-coherent sheaves on $U$ compatible with the gluing isomorphisms, i.e.

$$\psi \circ p_1^* h \;=\; p_2^* h \circ \varphi .$$

Descent data on $U/X$ therefore forms a category, denoted $\mathrm{Desc}(U/X)$ in the statement below.

Example — Zariski Covers

For a Zariski cover $U = \bigsqcup_i U_i \to X$ of a scheme $X$, descent data along $f$ is exactly gluing data: quasi-coherent sheaves $\mathcal{F}_i$ on the pieces together with isomorphisms $\varphi_{ij} : \mathcal{F}_i|_{U_{ij}} \to \mathcal{F}_j|_{U_{ij}}$ on the overlaps satisfying $\varphi_{jk}\varphi_{ij} = \varphi_{ik}$ on the triple overlaps. The conclusion is the familiar statement that a quasi-coherent sheaf on $X$ is the same thing as compatible quasi-coherent sheaves on the pieces — which is why Zariski descent is unremarkable and the theorem below is not.

Example — Galois Descent

Let $L/k$ be a finite Galois extension with group $G$ and take $U = \operatorname{Spec} L \to \operatorname{Spec} k = X$. A quasi-coherent sheaf on $\operatorname{Spec} L$ is a vector space $V$ over $L$, and descent data is an action of $G$ on $V$ by semilinear maps: a collection of isomorphisms $g : V \to V$ satisfying the cocycle condition $gh = g \circ h$. The descent theorem below then asserts that

$$\{\text{descending data on } \operatorname{Spec} L / \operatorname{Spec} k\} \;\simeq\; \{\text{vector spaces over } k\},$$

recovering classical Galois descent. This is Exercise 3 in §8; it is the single best example to carry in mind for everything that follows.

§ 5

The fppf Descent Theorem for Quasi-Coherent Sheaves

Theorem — fppf Descent for Quasi-Coherent Sheaves

Let $f : U \to X$ be an fppf cover (faithfully flat and locally of finite presentation). Then the pullback functor

$$f^* : \mathbf{QCoh}(X) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{Desc}(U/X)$$

sending a quasi-coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ to $(f^*\mathcal{F}, \text{the canonical gluing isomorphism})$ is an equivalence of categories.

An equivalence is two statements, and they are proved by different means:

(1) Full faithfulness. For quasi-coherent $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}$ on $X$, the pullback map on morphism sets is a bijection. Equivalently, the sequence
(2) Essential surjectivity. Every descent datum on $U/X$ is isomorphic to the pullback of a quasi-coherent sheaf on $X$.

Full faithfulness is a statement about morphisms and reduces to an equalizer diagram; essential surjectivity is a statement about objects and requires a construction. We take them in turn.

Full faithfulness, in one line

We must show that

$$\operatorname{Hom}_X(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}) \;\longrightarrow\; \operatorname{Hom}_U(f^*\mathcal{F}, f^*\mathcal{G}) \;\rightrightarrows\; \operatorname{Hom}_{U\times_X U}(p_1^* f^* \mathcal{F}, p_2^* f^* \mathcal{G})$$

is an equalizer: a morphism downstairs is exactly a morphism on the cover whose two pullbacks to the overlap agree. Since $f$ is fppf, we may work Zariski-locally on $X$ and $U$ and reduce to the affine case $X = \operatorname{Spec} R$, $U = \operatorname{Spec} S$, where $\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G}$ correspond to $R$-modules $M, N$ and $f^*$ corresponds to $- \otimes_R S$. The assertion becomes: for a faithfully flat $R$-algebra $S$ of finite presentation and $R$-modules $M, N$, the diagram

$$\operatorname{Hom}_R(M, N) \;\longrightarrow\; \operatorname{Hom}_S(M \otimes_R S,\, N \otimes_R S) \;\rightrightarrows\; \operatorname{Hom}_{S \otimes_R S}\big(M \otimes_R (S \otimes_R S),\, N \otimes_R (S \otimes_R S)\big)$$

is an equalizer. This follows from the affine lemma of §6 applied to $M$ and to $N$, together with the exactness of $-\otimes_R S$: a map is determined by its base change because the first map is injective, and a compatible map on the cover descends because the middle exactness of the lemma supplies the needed element downstairs. This is where faithful flatness is used, and only there.

§ 6

An Affine Lemma: Faithfully Flat Maps and Equalizers

The whole of fppf descent rests on one algebraic fact, which is worth isolating because it is also the engine of Galois descent and of flat descent for modules.

Lemma — Faithfully Flat Base Change Is an Equalizer

Let $R \to S$ be a faithfully flat ring homomorphism and let $M$ be an $R$-module. Then the sequence

$$M \;\xrightarrow{\;d_0\;}\; M \otimes_R S \;\xrightarrow[\;d_2\;]{\;d_1\;}\; M \otimes_R S \otimes_R S$$

is exact, where $d_0(m) = m \otimes 1$, $d_1(m \otimes s) = m \otimes s \otimes 1$, and $d_2(m \otimes s) = m \otimes 1 \otimes s$. That is, $M$ is the equalizer of $d_1$ and $d_2$.

Proof sketch

Injectivity of $d_0$. If $m \neq 0$ in $M$, choose a linear functional $\lambda : M \to R$ with $\lambda(m) \neq 0$; then $m \otimes 1 = 0$ in $M \otimes_R S$ would force $\lambda(m) \otimes 1 = 0$ in $S$, contradicting faithful flatness.

Exactness in the middle. First suppose the map $R \to S$ splits, i.e. there is an $R$-module map $S \to R$ with $1 \mapsto 1$. Then the sequence is split exact, which is a formal computation with the two face maps. In general one reduces to the split case: $S \to S \otimes_R S$, $s \mapsto s \otimes 1$, is a faithfully flat base change that admits a section, namely multiplication $S \otimes_R S \to S$. Exactness can be checked after this base change because $-\otimes_R S$ is faithfully flat, and it holds there by the split case. (This is Exercise 1 in §8.)

□

Why this is the heart of descent

Everything in the theorem is a consequence of this one sequence. “Descent” is the statement that a module over $R$ is exactly a module over $S$ with a compatible isomorphism between its two pullbacks to $S \otimes_R S$ — the cocycle condition of §4 is the triple-overlap shadow of the same exactness.

§ 7

Sketch of Essential Surjectivity

It remains to build the descended object. Fix an fppf cover $U \to X$ and a descent datum $(\mathcal{F}, \varphi)$ on $U$.

Construction of the descended sheaf — affine case

First reduce to the case where $U \to X$ is an affine morphism, so that $X = \operatorname{Spec} R$, $U = \operatorname{Spec} S$ with $R \to S$ faithfully flat of finite presentation. The descent datum is an $S$-module $M$ together with an isomorphism of $S \otimes_R S$-modules

$$\varphi : M \otimes_S (S \otimes_R S) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; (S \otimes_R S) \otimes_S M$$

satisfying the cocycle condition on $S \otimes_R S \otimes_R S$. Set

$$N \;=\; \big\{ m \in M \;:\; \varphi(m \otimes 1) = 1 \otimes m \big\},$$

the descent of $M$: the elements of $M$ that are fixed by the gluing isomorphism. Then $N$ is an $R$-module, and one checks that the natural map $N \otimes_R S \to M$ is an isomorphism. The verification that $N \otimes_R S \to M$ is an isomorphism uses the affine lemma of §6 applied after the faithfully flat base change to $S$: the sequence acquires a section, hence splits, and the comparison map can be read off from the resulting decomposition. Independence of the construction from the choice of presentation is where the cocycle condition is used.

□

Remark — What is still missing

The reduction of the general case to the affine case, and the verification that the construction is independent of choices, are the technical parts. The same argument, run for sheaves of modules rather than modules, gives descent for sheaves I: for a cover $U \to X$, descent data on $U/X$ is fully faithful for the category of sheaves, and the construction above supplies the descended object when a presentation exists. The subtlety — in fact the whole reason fppf descent is a theorem rather than a tautology — is that for arbitrary sheaves the functor need not be essentially surjective; it is on quasi-coherent sheaves that the argument above applies.

§ 8

Exercises from Litt's Lecture 4

These are the exercises marked in Litt’s Lecture 4. They follow the order in which the ideas appeared above: the equalizer lemma first, then the reduction steps that make the descent theorem available, and finally Galois descent as the guiding example.

Exercise 1 — The Equalizer Lemma for Faithfully Flat Maps

Let $R \to S$ be a faithfully flat ring homomorphism and $M$ an $R$-module. Prove that

$$M \;\longrightarrow\; M \otimes_R S \;\rightrightarrows\; M \otimes_R S \otimes_R S$$

is an exact sequence. Hint: handle the case in which $R \to S$ splits, then reduce to it by base changing along $S \to S \otimes_R S$ and using that exactness can be checked after a faithfully flat base change.

Exercise 2 — Reduction to the Affine Case

Let $U \to X$ be an fppf cover. Show that in order to prove full faithfulness of $f^* : \mathbf{QCoh}(X) \to \mathrm{Desc}(U/X)$, it suffices to treat the case in which $X = \operatorname{Spec} R$ and $U = \operatorname{Spec} S$ are affine with $R \to S$ faithfully flat of finite presentation. What to check: that the statement is Zariski-local on $X$ and on $U$, and that restriction of quasi-coherent sheaves to an affine open commutes with the morphism sets involved.

Exercise 3 — Galois Descent as a Special Case

Let $L/k$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G$, and take the fppf cover $\operatorname{Spec} L \to \operatorname{Spec} k$.

  1. Show that descent data for a quasi-coherent sheaf on $\operatorname{Spec} L$ along this cover is the same as a $k$-vector space $V$ with a semilinear action of $G$, i.e. isomorphisms $g : V \to V$ of abelian groups with $g(\lambda v) = g(\lambda) g(v)$ for $\lambda \in L$, satisfying $(gh)(v) = g(h(v))$.
  2. Conclude that every such $(V, G\text{-action})$ comes from a $k$-vector space, so that the descent theorem recovers Galois descent.

Hint: the cocycle condition on the triple overlap is exactly the relation $(gh)(v) = g(h(v))$; average over $G$ to identify the descended subspace with the invariants $V^G$ when $L/k$ is finite.

Exercise 4 — Compatibility with the Zariski Case

Let $U = \bigsqcup_i U_i \to X$ be a Zariski cover. Check that descent data for quasi-coherent sheaves along this cover agrees with the usual gluing data

$$\varphi_{ij} : \mathcal{F}_i|_{U_{ij}} \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \mathcal{F}_j|_{U_{ij}}, \qquad \varphi_{jk}\varphi_{ij} = \varphi_{ik} \ \text{ on } U_{ijk},$$

so that the fppf descent theorem specializes to the statement that quasi-coherent sheaves glue in the Zariski topology — a statement we already know. Moral: a theorem of descent should be checked against the cases where there is nothing to prove.
