# Values

This seminar is inspired in part by questions raised in Terence Tao's 2026 essay [*Mathematics in the Age of AI*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16753) and by the recommendations he discusses from the Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics.

We use tools freely, but the seminar exists for work that tools do not replace: human judgment, mathematical checking, shared responsibility, and community.

## 1. Build and sustain mathematical community

Community is an explicit mathematical output of the seminar, not an accidental side effect. A useful meeting should leave participants better able to question, explain, review, and learn with one another.

## 2. Digestion over generation

Producing a proof, summary, or example is cheap. Understanding why it works, checking it, deciding whether it is illuminating, and incorporating it into shared knowledge is the harder task.

## 3. Disclose tool use

AI, computer algebra, proof assistants, search systems, and other tools are welcome. Participants should briefly disclose substantial tool use connected with mathematical contributions, especially when a tool helped generate examples, arguments, computations, or formulations.

## 4. Support the needs of reviewing

A contribution should be written so another participant can actually check it. State hypotheses, relevant definitions, sources, computations, and unclear points. Do not hide the difficult part behind polished prose.

## 5. Affirm the humanity of authorship

AI is a tool, not a member of the seminar. A human contributor is responsible for every mathematical claim they submit. Submitting an AI-generated answer without understanding or checking it is not a contribution.

## 6. Put effort into proper attribution

Give credit to Litt, Tsimerman, Milne, the Stacks Project, books, papers, other participants, or other identifiable sources when they materially supply an idea, example, proof, or formulation. AI does not replace attribution to mathematical sources.

## 7. Friction is information

Record where understanding actually broke down. A polished final answer should not erase useful information about where the mathematics is subtle.

## 8. Verification before promotion

Candidate mathematical content becomes part of the permanent toolkit only after human checking and discussion.

## 9. Community knowledge over individual display

The objective is not to demonstrate who knows the most. Advanced participants have a responsibility to make discussion more accessible rather than monopolize it.
